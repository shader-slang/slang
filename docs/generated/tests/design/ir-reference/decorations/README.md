# Tests for design/ir-reference/decorations

## Intent

This bundle is the per-opcode reference for the IR `Decoration`
family as catalogued in
[`decorations.md`](../../../../design/ir-reference/decorations.md). The doc holds 196 concrete
decoration opcodes, so the coverage strategy is a representative sample
rather than an enumeration: for each category section that has a natural
Slang surface, the tests compile the construct that ought to produce the
decoration and FileCheck the `-dump-ir` snapshot for the printed
`[opcode(operands)]` line, anchored at a user-named symbol so the
core-module preamble - which carries its own copies of `BuiltinDecoration`,
`readNone` and `targetIntrinsic` - cannot satisfy the match. Every
`-dump-ir` test feeds thread-id-dependent operands and sinks its result
into an `RWStructuredBuffer`, so neither constant folding nor DCE removes
the decorated declaration before the CHECK sees it. Claims the doc states
in terms of what the _backend_ does with a hint (`branch`, `flatten`,
`loopControl`) are observed at final emit instead, on both HLSL and SPIR-V,
because that is where the doc says the hint is consumed. Boundary probes
sit on the operand-bearing decorations - workgroup dimensions, wave sizes,
locations, interpolation modes, semantic indices, loop-control modes - and
the negative tests pin the diagnostics that fire before a decoration is
ever created. Decorations whose `AST origin` column reads `(synthesized)`
are out of scope by construction and are recorded in
`## Untested claims`, as are the categories that need a non-compute stage,
a second module, or a downstream toolchain.

## Claims

The doc's testable claims are its per-category opcode tables plus the
`Notable opcodes` write-ups. They decompose along three axes, and every
row of `## Functional coverage` and `## Untested claims` above names the
specific opcode it belongs to:

1. **Which AST surface produces which decoration.** Each table row pairs
   an `AST origin` with an `Opcode`; the claim is that writing that
   surface makes that decoration appear on the lowered inst.
2. **What the operands are.** Each row's `Operands` column commits to an
   arity and to operand kinds - string literals print quoted, integer
   literals print as `N : Int`, inst references print as `%N`, and a
   nullary decoration prints bare.
3. **What the decoration is attached to.** A decoration prints on the
   line immediately preceding the inst it decorates, and several claims
   are specifically about _which_ inst that is: `entryPoint` only on the
   entry-point function, `shader64BitIndexing` lifted onto the entry
   point even when the attribute was written on a callee, `nodeArraySize`
   and `allowSparseNodes` on the output-array parameter rather than on
   the node function, `BuiltinRequirementDecoration` on the requirement
   key rather than on the interface entry.

The `Notable opcodes` sections add four cross-cutting claims that are
covered separately above: control-flow hints carry no IR semantics and
are consumed at emit; `layout` is a link to a separate `Layout` opcode
rather than a container; `keepAlive` keeps insts alive that no use chain
reaches; and `targetIntrinsic` can appear several times on one function,
one per target.

## Sibling-bundle overlap

`design/cross-cutting/ir-instructions` samples the decoration family once
as one IR family among many - it covers `nameHint`, `entryPoint`,
`targetIntrinsic`, `nodeLaunch`, the `builtinRequirementKey` pairing and
the two `glslFragDepth*` emit observations. This bundle drills the same
family per-opcode and per-category instead, so the overlap is
deliberate and narrow: where the sibling asserts "this family exists and
this row works", the tests here pin operand arity, operand kinds, the
attachment point, and the boundary values of every operand-bearing
decoration sampled.

`design/ir-reference/structure` observes a couple of decorations in
passing on their structural parents (a `nameHint` on a `func`, an
`export` on a key); the linkage tests here instead pin the four
symbol-kind prefixes of the exported mangled name.
