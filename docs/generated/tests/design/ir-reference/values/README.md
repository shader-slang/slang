# Tests for design/ir-reference/values

## Intent

This bundle is the per-opcode regression suite for the IR value-producing
family catalogued in
[`docs/generated/design/ir-reference/values.md`](../../../../design/ir-reference/values.md):
literals and their inline payload encoding, arithmetic and bitwise
operators, the non-short-circuiting logical opcodes and the branch-free
`select`, comparisons, the `emitCast` conversion table, memory and field
access, aggregate constructors and projections, the swizzle trio, and the
`Optional` / `Result` helpers. The strategy is one claim per catalog row
plus the boundary probes each row's operand domain implies: for a numeric
opcode that means the documented `MIN` / `MAX` / zero / overflow edges of
every type the row applies to; for a conversion opcode it means every
(from, to) pair the `emitCast` table admits at the type ranks Slang
exposes; for an indexed opcode it means the lower edge, the upper edge,
and the rejected out-of-range case.

The primary observation point is `-target <text-target> -dump-ir
-o /dev/null`, FileChecked against the IR dump, because nearly every claim
in this doc is about which opcode lowering emits rather than about emitted
target text. Patterns are anchored at `func %main` or a user-named callee
so the large IR preamble cannot satisfy them, operands come from `uniform`
globals or `SV_DispatchThreadID` so the constant folder has nothing to
collapse, and every observed value is stored into an `RWStructuredBuffer`
so dead-code elimination cannot remove it. Where a value opcode genuinely
has target-divergent user-visible text — vector swizzle, literal
emission, the signedness fan-out of `div` / `shr` / `irem` / `cmpLT` /
`cmpGE` / `intCast` / `castFloatToInt` in SPIR-V — the claim also carries
per-target `SIMPLE` emission directives with a distinct CHECK prefix each,
since the doc makes no cross-target uniformity claim for those.

Catalog rows already sampled by
[`cross-cutting/ir-instructions`](../../cross-cutting/ir-instructions/README.md)
(`add` / `sub` / `mul` / `div` on `int`, `cmpGT`, `intCast` from `uint`,
the `var` + `load` + `store` + `get_field_addr` quartet on a local struct,
single-component `swizzle`, `rwstructuredBufferGetElementPtr`) are not
re-smoke-tested here; this bundle drills into the rest of the catalog and
into the boundaries of the shared rows.

## Claims

Enumerated per [`_claims.md` §1](../../../_meta/prompts/_claims.md), grouped by the
source doc's own headings. The page is a per-opcode catalog, so most claims are
table rows of the form "AST surface X lowers to IR opcode Y with operand shape Z";
those are user-observable through `-dump-ir`. A minority describe compiler-internal
structure with no user-visible consequence (the Lua-to-enumerator naming rules, the
C++ wrapper hierarchy, deduplication identity, the `Detailed` dump mode) and are
collected separately at the end as **internal-source facts**; those are classified in
`## Untested claims` rather than tested.

**Literals (`Constant` group)**

1. Two `IntLit 42` produce the same IR value: the `Constant` opcodes are deduplicated through the constant map rather than by the hoistable flag.
2. Each literal stores its payload (integer, float, bytes, ...) inline on the `IRInst`, not in the operand list.
3. `boolConst` comes from a `BoolLiteralExpr` and encodes `true` / `false`.
4. `integer_constant` comes from an `IntegerLiteralExpr` (and from `ConstantIntVal` via `lowerVal`), with signedness encoded in the result type.
5. `float_constant` comes from a `FloatingPointLiteralExpr`.
6. `ptr_constant` comes from a `NullPtrLiteralExpr` such as `nullptr`.
7. `void_constant` is the unique `void` value, produced by a `NoneLiteralExpr` and wherever a cast to `void` is discarded.
8. `string_constant` comes from a `StringLiteralExpr` and stores its bytes inline.
9. `blob_constant` is an arbitrary blob literal whose only caller is `emitEmbeddedDownstreamIR`.

**Undefined and default-construct**

10. `LoadFromUninitializedMemory` is synthesized by SSA construction when a variable is read on a path with no reaching store; frontend diagnostics surface its uses.
11. `Poison` is an infectious undefined value, hoistable so all poison values of one type dedupe, synthesized by legalization and autodiff passes with no single owning pass.
12. `defaultConstruct` is nullary and produces a default-initialized value of its result type, from a `DefaultConstructExpr` or `getDefaultVal`.

**Arithmetic and bitwise**

13. There is no `InfixExpr` / `PrefixExpr` visitor: an operator is an overloaded call that reaches its opcode either as a `BuiltinOperatorExpr` (bypassing call lowering) or through `emitCallToDeclRef` reading the core module's `__intrinsic_op`.
14. `+` lowers to `add`.
15. `-` (binary) lowers to `sub`.
16. `*` lowers to `mul`.
17. `/` lowers to `div`, with signed / unsigned / floating-point behaviour keyed by operand types.
18. `%` with an integer element type lowers to `irem`, which is remainder and _not_ modulus.
19. `%` with a floating-point element type lowers to `frem`, chosen over `irem` by inspecting the element type of the first argument.
20. Unary `-` lowers to `neg`.
21. `<<` lowers to `shl`.
22. `>>` lowers to `shr`, arithmetic or logical chosen by operand signedness.
23. `&` lowers to `and`.
24. `|` lowers to `or`.
25. `^` lowers to `xor`.
26. `~` lowers to `bitnot`.
27. `!` lowers to `not`.
28. The core-module `bitfieldExtract(value, offset, count)` lowers to `bitfieldExtract`, extracting into the low bits of the result.
29. The core-module `bitfieldInsert(base, insert, offset, count)` lowers to `bitfieldInsert`.

**Logical**

30. `logicalAnd` / `logicalOr` are _not_ the lowering of `&&` / `||`: those check to a `LogicOperatorShortCircuitExpr` and lower to an `ifElse` plus a join-block `Param`.
31. `logicalAnd` comes from the core-module `and` / `IBool::and` and does not short-circuit.
32. `logicalOr` comes from the core-module `or` / `IBool::or` and does not short-circuit.
33. `select(condition, trueResult, falseResult)` is the branch-free conditional, reached from a `SelectExpr` whose condition is not a `BasicExpressionType` (or at global scope) and from the `__intrinsic_op(select)` on `operator?:` / `select()`.

**Comparison**

34. `==` lowers to `cmpEQ`.
35. `!=` lowers to `cmpNE`.
36. `<` lowers to `cmpLT`.
37. `<=` lowers to `cmpLE`.
38. `>` lowers to `cmpGT`.
39. `>=` lowers to `cmpGE`.
40. `cmpNE` is also emitted by `emitCast` for a float-to-`bool` cast, comparing against `defaultConstruct`.

**Conversions**

41. Most numeric conversions are chosen by `emitCast` through a 5x5 table over the data-carrying `TypeCastStyle` values `Int`, `Float`, `Bool`, `Ptr`, `Enum`.
42. A table entry may name two opcodes, so the cast lowers to a pair — `Ptr` to `Float` becomes `CastPtrToInt` followed by `CastIntToFloat`.
43. The `Bool` to `Bool` entry is a `Nop`: the operand is returned unchanged.
44. The `Float` to `Bool` entry is a `cmpNE` against `defaultConstruct`.
45. Casts to `void` never reach the table.
46. `BuiltinCast` comes from a `BuiltinCastExpr` and is the fallback when either side's `TypeCastStyle` is `Unknown`.
47. `bitCast` comes from the core-module `bit_cast<T, U>` and the other `__intrinsic_op($(kIROp_BitCast))` declarations, and is also the `Ptr` to `Ptr` table entry.
48. `asuint` / `asint` / `asfloat` / `asdouble` are _not_ a surface for `bitCast`: declared with `__intrinsic_asm`, they stay `call` instructions and are resolved by the target-intrinsic mechanism at emit.
49. `reinterpret` comes from the core-module `reinterpret<T, U>` and is less restrictive than `bitCast`.
50. `ReinterpretOptional` has no producer at `source_commit`.
51. `unmodified` comes from the core-module `unused` / `unmodified` and is a no-op cast that silences the uninitialized-use warning.
52. `outImplicitCast` comes from an `OutImplicitCastExpr` materialized by `tryGetAddress`; its operand is the _address_ of the caller's variable and its result type is a `Ptr`.
53. `inOutImplicitCast` is the `inout` / borrowed-`inout` counterpart with the same pointer-in / pointer-out shape.
54. `intCast` is the Int-to-Int, Int-to-Bool and Bool-to-Int cell, with sign or zero extension chosen by the types.
55. `floatCast` is the Float-to-Float cell (precision change).
56. `castIntToFloat` is the Int-to-Float and Bool-to-Float cell.
57. `castFloatToInt` is the Float-to-Int cell and truncates.
58. `CastPtrToBool` is true if the pointer operand is non-null.
59. `CastPtrToInt` reinterprets a pointer as an integer.
60. `CastIntToPtr` reinterprets an integer as a pointer.
61. `castToVoid` is declared but never emitted; `(void)expr` produces a `void_constant` instead.
62. `PtrCast` casts between pointer types of different element types and has no producer at `source_commit`.
63. `CastEnumToInt` is the Enum-to-Int cell and the first half of Enum-to-Float / Enum-to-Bool / Enum-to-Ptr.
64. `CastIntToEnum` is the Int-to-Enum and Bool-to-Enum cell and the second half of Float-to-Enum / Ptr-to-Enum.
65. `EnumCast` is the Enum-to-Enum cell, but an enum-to-enum conversion written in Slang source never reaches it: the checker builds two nested `BuiltinCastExpr` nodes, so lowering emits a `CastEnumToInt` followed by a `CastIntToEnum`.
66. `CastUInt2ToDescriptorHandle` comes from `DescriptorHandle<T>.__init(uint2)`.
67. `CastDescriptorHandleToUInt2` comes from `uint2.__init(DescriptorHandle<T>)`.
68. `CastUInt64ToDescriptorHandle` comes from `DescriptorHandle<T>.__init(uint64_t)`.
69. `CastDescriptorHandleToUInt64` comes from `uint64_t.__init(DescriptorHandle<T>)`.
70. `CastDescriptorHandleToResource` comes from `__castDescriptorHandleToResource<T>` and is also synthesized by Metal parameter-block lowering.
71. `CastResourceToDescriptorHandle` has no core-module spelling and is produced only by Metal parameter-block lowering.

**Memory**

72. `var` comes from a local `VarDecl` plus temporaries created throughout lowering, and its result type is `Ptr<T>`.
73. `global_var` is the module-scope mutable variable, documented in `structure.md`.
74. `globalConstant` comes from a module-scope `const` / `static const` `VarDecl`.
75. `alloca` allocates stack storage sized from an RTTI object and has no producer at `source_commit`.
76. `load(ptr)` comes from a `DerefExpr` and from every rvalue read of an lvalue; operands beyond the first are attributes, not values.
77. `store(ptr, val)` comes from assignment lowering.
78. `copyLogical(dest, srcPtr)` copies a whole value between two _pointers_ member by member without reinterpreting bytes, has result type `void`, and is produced by buffer-element-type legalization.
79. `get_field(base, field)` comes from a `MemberExpr` on a value (rvalue path); `field` is a `StructKey`.
80. `get_field_addr(base, field)` comes from a `MemberExpr` on an lvalue and returns the member's address.
81. `getElement(base, index)` comes from an `IndexExpr` on a value.
82. `getElementPtr(base, index)` comes from an `IndexExpr` on an lvalue.
83. `getOffsetPtr(base, offset)` comes from the core-module `__getOffsetPtr` / pointer `operator+` and offsets by a count of elements.
84. `getAddr` has no producer: `__getAddress(...)` lowers to `assumeAddress` instead.
85. `assumeAddress` marks an address obtained via `__getAddress` so IR validation can reject cases the target disallows, and is lowered away to its operand after validation.
86. `swizzle(base, index0, ...)` comes from a `SwizzleExpr`, and each index is an integer literal.
87. `swizzleSet(base, source, index0, ...)` comes from assignment to a swizzle lvalue whose base has no address; its indices start at operand 2.
88. `swizzledStore(dest, source, index0, ...)` comes from assignment to a swizzle lvalue when the destination is addressable.
89. `matrixSwizzleStore(dest, source, (row, col)...)` comes from assignment to a matrix-swizzle lvalue, and its trailing indices come in _pairs_.
90. `updateElement` is a synthesized functional update returning a copy of `base` with one nested element replaced.

**Strings and native pointers**

91. `makeString` constructs a `String` from a `NativeString`.
92. `getNativeStr` returns an unowned `NativeString` view of a `String`.
93. `getNativePtr` returns a native pointer from a `ComPtr<T>` / interface / `ExtractExistentialType` value.
94. `getManagedPtrWriteRef` returns a write reference to a managed-pointer variable.
95. `ManagedPtrAttach` attaches a managed-pointer variable to a `NativePtr` without changing its reference count.
96. `ManagedPtrDetach` detaches a managed-pointer variable from its `NativePtr`.

**Object and CUDA helpers**

97. `allocObj` allocates an object value and is used by host-side and managed-pointer lowering.
98. `CUDA_LDG` is the read-only cached load introduced by the CUDA immutable-load pass.

**Aggregate constructors**

99. The `make*` opcodes have two producers: `visitInitializerListExpr` / `getDefaultVal` for `{ ... }` syntax, and the core-module constructors declared with `__intrinsic_op`, which is how `float3(x, y, z)` becomes a `makeVector`.
100. `makeVector(components...)` constructs a vector from its components.
101. `makeMatrix(components...)` constructs a matrix from rows or scalars.
102. `makeMatrixFromScalar(scalarVal)` splats a scalar into a matrix.
103. `MakeVectorFromScalar` splats a scalar into a vector and takes a _single_ operand, the element type and count coming from the result type.
104. `makeArray(elements...)` constructs a fixed-size array.
105. `makeArrayFromElement(element)` splats a single element into a fixed-size array, and is also what `getDefaultVal` uses for array types.
106. `makeCoopVector(components...)` constructs a cooperative-vector value.
107. `makeCoopVectorFromValuePack(valuePack)` constructs a cooperative-vector value from a value pack.
108. `makeCoopMatrixFromScalar` is named by no `__intrinsic_op`, so it never arises from a call written in Slang source.
109. `makeStruct(fieldValues...)` constructs a struct from its field values in declaration order.
110. `makeTuple(elements...)` constructs a tuple.
111. `makeTargetTuple(elements...)` builds the target-keyed tuple used by `targetSwitch`.
112. `makeValuePack(elements...)` constructs a hoistable value-pack aggregate from a `PackExpr`, so identical packs dedupe.
113. `makeCombinedTextureSampler(texture, sampler)` bundles a texture and a sampler.
114. `makeUInt64(low, high)` constructs a `uint64` from two `uint32` halves; there is no `MakeUInt64Expr` AST node.
115. `SumVectorElements` sums all elements of a vector and is introduced by autodiff transposition.
116. `SumMatrixElements` sums all elements of a matrix and is introduced by autodiff transposition.

**Reshape and pack helpers**

117. `matrixReshape(matrix)` reshapes a matrix to a different row / column count with the same element type.
118. `vectorReshape(vector)` reshapes a vector and is synthesized.
119. `getTupleElement(tuple, elementIndex)` is emitted only by `visitEachExpr`; positional member access is not a second surface, because `t._0` is rewritten into a `swizzle`.
120. `getTargetTupleElement` reads one element of a target tuple and is synthesized.

**Result / Optional / Conditional helpers**

121. `makeResultValue(value)` constructs a `Result` holding a success value.
122. `makeResultError(errorValue)` constructs a `Result` holding an error value.
123. `isResultError(result)` is true if the `Result` holds an error.
124. `getResultValue(result)` reads the success value.
125. `getResultError(result)` reads the error value.
126. `makeOptionalValue(value)` constructs an `Optional<T>` from a value.
127. `makeOptionalNone` constructs an `Optional<T>` with no value.
128. `optionalHasValue(optional)` is true if the optional holds a value.
129. `getOptionalValue(optional)` reads the value.
130. `makeConditionalValue(value)` constructs a `Conditional` value.
131. `getConditionalValue(conditional)` reads the inner value of a `Conditional`.
132. `extractTaggedUnionTag` / `extractTaggedUnionPayload` sit next to these in the Lua file but belong to the existential representation tabulated on `generics-and-existentials.md`.

**Constexpr arithmetic and casts**

133. The whole `constexpr*` family (`constexprAdd` through `constexprEnumCast`) mirrors the runtime arithmetic and cast opcodes, is hoistable so identical compile-time values dedupe, and is produced by `lowerVal` walking `IntVal` subclasses rather than by any expression visitor.
134. `BuiltinOperationKind::Mod` becomes `constexprIRem`, never a `constexprFRem` (which does not exist, since an `IntVal` is integral by construction).
135. `BuiltinOperationKind::Conditional` becomes `constexprSelect`.
136. `constexprAnd` / `constexprOr` _are_ produced from `&&` / `||` inside a constant expression — the opposite of the runtime `logicalAnd` / `logicalOr` situation — because there is no control flow to short-circuit through in an `IntVal`.
137. `emitConstexprCast` picks the typed `constexpr*Cast` op from a 4x4 table, four styles rather than five because `Ptr` and `Void` cannot appear in an `IntVal`.

**Literal payload encoding**

138. A pass author who inspects operands directly sees _zero_ operands on a literal and must call the typed accessors on the wrapper class.
139. `shouldFoldInstIntoUses` folds every `IRConstant` into its use sites, so a literal never gets a definition line of its own and is printed inline wherever it is used.
140. The inline spelling is `<value> : <Type>` for `IntLit` and `FloatLit`.
141. The inline spelling is a bare `true` / `false` for `BoolLit`, with no type clause.
142. The inline spelling is the quoted bytes for `StringLit`.
143. The inline spelling is `<ptr>` for `PtrLit`.
144. The inline spelling is `<binary blob>` for `BlobLit`.
145. `VoidLit` has no case in that switch and falls through to the generic form, which prints the opcode name `void_constant`.
146. `IRDumpOptions::Mode::Detailed` is the one exception to the folding, turning it off so the dump mirrors the instruction list exactly.

**`Var` vs `GlobalVar` vs `Alloca`**

147. `var` declares a local variable inside a function whose result type is `Ptr<T>`; `global_var` is the module-scope counterpart; ordinary value-typed locals always become `var`.
148. `alloca` is _not_ the dynamically-sized form of `var`: its single operand is an RTTI object pointer and the C++ backend emits `alloca(<operand>->typeSize)`, reading the size out of the RTTI object at run time.

**`FieldAddress` vs `FieldExtract`, `GetElementPtr` vs `GetElement`**

149. `get_field_addr` returns a pointer so the result can flow into another `store` / `get_field_addr` / `getElementPtr`, whereas `get_field` returns the member's _value_ and breaks the lvalue chain; lowering picks one by whether the `MemberExpr` is in lvalue or rvalue position.
150. The same distinction holds for array indexing: `getElementPtr` returns a pointer to the element and `getElement` returns the element's value.

**`swizzle`, `swizzleSet`, `swizzledStore`**

151. `swizzle base idx0 idx1 ...` reads M elements from an N-vector to make an M-vector.
152. `swizzleSet base source idx0 ...` takes a vector and a smaller value pack and returns a new vector where the selected lanes have been overwritten.
153. `swizzledStore` mutates memory in place and has the same `dest, source, indices...` shape.
154. `matrixSwizzleStore` is the one member of the group whose trailing operands are _pairs_, so a reader who assumes one index per element will double-count.
155. Which of `swizzleSet` and `swizzledStore` an assignment produces is decided by whether the swizzle's _base_ has an address: `tryGetAddress` yielding a pointer gives `swizzledStore` (or a plain `store` through `getElementPtr` when only one lane is selected), and otherwise lowering loads the base, applies `swizzleSet`, and assigns the updated value back.
156. A base without an address is a `property` or `__subscript` with `get` / `set` accessors but no `ref` accessor, so `s.P.xy = float2(1, 2)` gives get / `swizzleSet` / set while `s.v.xy = float2(1, 2)` gives `swizzledStore`.

**`updateElement`**

157. `updateElement` builds `base`, then `newElement`, then an arbitrarily long _access chain_ of struct keys and element indices, so the surrounding code stays in SSA form instead of needing a `var` plus `store`.

**`select`**

158. `select` is an _expression_: it produces a value and does not affect control flow, both result operands must already be computed, and their types must match.
159. A scalar `SelectExpr` inside a function lowers to an `ifElse` with `then` / `else` blocks and a join-block `Param`, because Slang's scalar `?:` short-circuits even though HLSL's does not.

**`Poison`**

160. A poison value of type T behaves like an out-of-band T-NaN: most instructions that consume a poison operand yield a poison result, the exceptions being `select` and block parameters, which can pass a poison operand through on a non-taken edge without poisoning the result.

**`defaultConstruct`**

161. `defaultConstruct` is often _avoided_ rather than emitted: `emitDefaultConstruct` recurses into the result type first and builds a concrete aggregate where it can — `makeStruct` for a struct, `MakeVectorFromScalar` for a vector, `makeMatrixFromScalar` for a matrix, `makeOptionalNone` for an `Optional<T>`.
162. The types it cannot decompose are an opaque resource or sampler type, an interface-typed value, an unspecialized generic type parameter, and an associated type.
163. Two more shapes that look decomposable join them: an array or cooperative vector whose element count is not an `IRIntLit`, and one whose literal count exceeds the 4096-element cap.
164. `fallback` defaults to `true`, so all of those cases do produce the opcode rather than returning null to the caller.

**`MakeUInt64`**

165. `makeUInt64` exists because several targets have no direct `uint64` literal form, so the IR carries the two halves as explicit operands and the backend emits either a single literal or the halves combined at run time.

**`out` / `inout` argument casts**

166. An argument whose type already matches the parameter produces no cast: the caller's `var` pointer is passed straight through.
167. The opcodes appear only for an l-value argument of a _different_ type the checker will round-trip through a temporary, and `_canLValueCoerceScalarType` accepts only two integer types of the same width (or the vector / matrix forms of such a pair), so `int` / `uint` is close to the whole surface.
168. A type difference the coercion does not cover is rejected at semantic check before lowering runs.

**Descriptor-handle conversions**

169. `DescriptorHandle<T>` is an opaque bindless handle, and six opcodes in three pairs convert to and from it.
170. The 64-bit pack / unpack pair is gated on `spvBindlessTextureNV` and `cuda`, which is why the `uint2` pair exists alongside it for targets that spell a handle as two 32-bit words.
171. `CastDescriptorHandleToResource` has a core-module spelling so it can appear directly from user or core-module code, while `CastResourceToDescriptorHandle` has none.

**Live-but-unproduced opcodes**

172. Five opcodes are fully declared but nothing constructs them at `source_commit`: `castToVoid`, `PtrCast`, `getAddr`, `alloca`, and `ReinterpretOptional`.
173. `castToVoid` is the most likely to mislead because `void`'s `__init(T)` names it, but `emitCallToDeclRef` and `emitCast` both intercept and return `getVoidValue()`; the operand's side effects are preserved because they are already separate instructions.

**Internal-source facts** (no user-observable consequence; classified in `## Untested claims`)

174. Each Lua entry generates the enumerator `kIROp_` plus the entry's `struct_name`, which is not always the Lua key (`boolConst` becomes `kIROp_BoolLit`, `shl` becomes `kIROp_Lsh`, `and` becomes `kIROp_BitAnd`, `logicalAnd` becomes `kIROp_And`).
175. Every opcode on the page has an `IR<struct_name>` wrapper struct, and hand-written and FIDDLE-generated wrappers are mutually exclusive by construction rather than by a static exclusion list.
176. A generated wrapper's accessors derive from the Lua `operands` list, so an entry declaring no operand names yields a wrapper with no accessors and consumers must call `getOperand(i)`.
177. A hand-written wrapper ignores the Lua operand names entirely, so where the two disagree the wrapper wins.
178. The Lua file declares only three grouping parents in this family — `Constant`, `Undefined`, and `StoreBase` — and the remaining boxes in the hierarchy diagram are editorial groupings.
179. The rows for the live-but-unproduced opcodes are kept rather than deleted because the opcodes are live in the enum and in the stable-name table.
